# Xenium presentation production

## Build the argument before the slides

For a template-only rebuild, reuse the approved account and cues and continue at
assembly. Reopen content planning when the audience, scope or evidence changes.

Read the project evidence and meeting context. Establish the audience, what they
already know, their central question, and what the presenter wants them to
understand or decide. Separate documented priorities from planning assumptions.
Preserve the agreed duration, language, template and any fixed slide count.

Write the account as a short, connected narrative before dividing it into slides.
Choose its starting point from the meeting purpose. For a progress briefing,
begin with the received material and completed work; explain a check's rationale
beside its result when that helps the audience understand it. Select the few observations
and implications needed to reach the intended understanding. A descriptive update
can establish the current state and its limits without recommending an intervention.
Input consistency, an empty alert list or a favorable metric alone does not
establish overall data quality.

Treat the user's requested coverage as a responsibility of the whole talk. Reasons,
work performed, findings and their meaning can unfold across pages. Combine related
checks and omit secondary completed work when that makes the account clearer.
Pagination serves this narrative; each page can supply just one part of an
explanation. Keep sources and qualifications with the claims they support, without
turning the content brief into a repeated form for every slide.

Reuse a user-approved outline when revising content; challenge the revision
against the agreed brief rather than reopening settled choices. When an outline
is still needed and independent agents are available, give one the original
request, project context and QC/analysis evidence before selecting it. Ask what listeners
should understand by the end, which evidence earns speaking time, and how the
account should unfold. Have it propose content and likely audience questions,
then pagination and timing. Leave it free to choose a structure rather than
preserve an existing deck. The author checks its proposal against sources and
scope. If independent work is unavailable, identify this pass as self-review;
this is not another approval gate.

Draft titles after selecting the narrative. A requested topic cover identifies
the subject, presenter and occasion. For descriptive progress briefings, use plain
subject or activity titles to help listeners navigate; place findings and counts
in the body. Use conclusion titles when the meeting purpose or user preference
calls for them. Preserve uncertainty when it changes the meaning. Read the sequence with the planned displays and
transitions: listeners should be able to follow the account without already
knowing the dataset. Related panels in a finalized figure may form one evidence
unit. Keep secondary detail in the Q&A companion or an agreed appendix.

Before assembly, have the independent planner describe what a listener would take
away from the whole talk and challenge its emphasis, connections and omissions.
Resolve misleading impressions, repeated explanations and consequential questions
left unanswered. Then check individual claims against their evidence. Retain
consequential selection decisions with the build source. Passing every page's
local checks does not establish that the presentation works as a whole.

## Report completed data checks

For a data-status briefing, show what was received, what was actually checked and
what was observed. Put proposed work and requested inputs in next steps, using
current correspondence to distinguish new requests from existing follow-up. Explain
necessary terms beside the relevant evidence; a general methods lesson or a
standalone rationale page is useful only when it serves the requested purpose.

Name the sender, shared folder and relevant source date when describing receipt.
Describe sharing scope as specifically as the source permits. A statement that a
few files were shared does not define a cohort subset or its fraction of a total.

Make missing-file findings actionable: show recognizable sample/region and slide
identifiers, the missing file types, the locations actually checked and the effect
on use. Keep long exact paths in linked supporting material. Separate files found
missing in the shared source from questions that still require the sender's check.

Keep units, denominators and coverage needed to read a result. Replace abstract
qualifications with a specific observation or move the concrete information request
to next steps. Every displayed sentence should identify an action, result, useful
definition or requested input. Presenter commentary and general assurances of
caution do not substitute for those facts. Do not add a reassuring quality verdict
when the completed checks only establish particular measurements or consistency.

For a general collaborator overview, select metrics that answer recognizable
questions: how much data arrived, how much has the required analysis inputs, and
how much information is detected per cell. Choose a few measures and explain what
to look at and the observed takeaway beside the figure. Subject titles do not
replace this explanation. Detailed assay diagnostics can remain in supporting
material unless a specific finding makes them central. Request an appropriate
summary/figure from its owner when existing technical plots miss that purpose.

## Make the evidence visible

Build each page around the selected final figure, comparison table or explanatory
diagram, as appropriate to its claim. Let content determine the layout; preserve
a simple template without forcing every page into equal boxes or the same table.
Use short labels around the evidence. Keep an interpretation-critical denominator,
coverage limit or unknown next to the result it qualifies, rather than only in
notes. A distracted listener should be able to find both the point and its basis.

Distinguish observations, hypotheses and proposed actions. Match the ending to
the meeting purpose: a descriptive briefing may need only a brief outlook, leaving
detailed plans for discussion; a decision meeting may need explicit options and
requests. Identify proposals as proposals. Reuse project wording for units and identities.
Show a short source credit on the page containing borrowed evidence or a reported
external claim; retain full citations and internal file provenance separately in
notes or build records. A references-only ending is optional, not automatic.

Allocate speaking time by explanatory load. Allow time to orient listeners to a
scientific figure and to discuss its implication. Page-per-minute heuristics are
planning aids, not a fixed quota. Cut or split competing ideas before reducing
text size. Keep the live talk concise; a circulated report has a different purpose.

## Rebuild from saved inputs

When a template is supplied or configured in the project, use
[template-driven rebuilding](template-rebuild.md). Retain the template and its
profile separately from the ordered content, cues and final-figure references.
Use the bundled builder and the project rebuild command; put repairs in their
owning inputs or reusable code so the next run reproduces them. The final PPTX is
a generated artifact. Reuse established checks on routine rebuilds.

## Assemble finalized figures

Use the available presentation library and existing runtime. On Codex, follow the
available presentation tool's authoring and validation instructions, with the
user's finalized-figure requirement taking precedence over native-chart defaults.
In another environment, choose its supported PPTX writer with the same behavior.

Insert the final PNG or a vector export the target application reliably supports.
Preserve the whole figure and aspect ratio. Place editable titles, explanation
and tables around it. The standalone plotting task owns scientific artwork,
including annotations: return any required figure change there, then consume its
new final export. Do not reconstruct plots or cover their contents in the deck.
Save the figure references and content with the build source.

## Speaker notes

Write glanceable speaking cues, typically three to five short lines, rather than
a prose script. Include the main point, how to read the display, and the essential
qualification or transition. Use English unless requested otherwise. Keep extended
answers in the Q&A companion; separate citations from the cues. Honor an explicit
request for a full script.

## Verify and deliver

First assess the complete account: does it leave the audience with the intended
understanding, with proportionate emphasis and clear connections? Then read titles
with their displays and check claim accuracy, consequential unknowns and coverage. Read cues aloud or
estimate a timed walkthrough; distinguish a timing plan from a rehearsed duration.

For a first build or affected layout changes, verify structure and notes, then
render and inspect affected pages for readability and fit. Confirm that inserted
figures match the declared final exports by direct byte/pixel comparison; calculate
checksums only on explicit request. If the library transcodes images, compare
decoded content. Separate rendering checks from testing in PowerPoint itself.

Retain the final PPTX and build source. State limitations that affect use. An
outline, figure bundle or skill revision does not complete a requested PPTX.

## Basis

This workflow adapts Minto's [single governing point](https://www.barbaraminto.com/),
Naegle's [message, scope and audience-readability guidance](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009554),
and the academic-pptx repository's [title-sequence and exhibit checks](https://github.com/gabberflast/academic-pptx-skill/blob/9f2b703ffe8d1449851617665ab1ffb3516d54ac/content_guidelines.md).
These sources inform communication choices; they do not validate Xenium quality
criteria or require a universal slide template. The instructions above are
self-contained; opening those sources is optional background reading. Source
limits and adaptation choices are recorded in the
[design rationale](../../../docs/presentation-design.md).
