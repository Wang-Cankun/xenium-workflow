# Presentation design: evidence and adaptation

The presentation branch is owned by
[presentation.md](../skills/xenium-reporting/references/presentation.md).
This record explains its design choices; it is not a second runtime checklist.

## Observed problem

An audience-question planning pass still produced a deck organized by analysis
categories. Topic headings named what was examined but left the listener to infer
the answer. A supplemental-metrics page mixed several unrelated measurements;
qualifications were repeated while the argument remained implicit. The repair is
at content selection and claim–evidence alignment, before styling. Attractive
layout and complete topic coverage cannot establish that the story works.

A subsequent user correction exposed a second problem: a list of whole-talk needs
was becoming a form applied to every page. The revised branch develops a connected
account before pagination and reviews the listener's overall understanding before
local claim/display accuracy. A page may provide context, evidence or interpretation
for a later page; related checks can be combined and secondary work omitted. The
meeting purpose determines whether the close is a brief outlook or a decision request.

## Adaptation decisions

The connected narrative, title sequence and independent whole-talk challenge apply
these ideas to an observational research update. Titles state supported findings
or explicit limits. Descriptive evidence never needs to become a causal claim,
a quality pass/fail decision or an unrequested biological recommendation.
Necessary setup pages can keep neutral titles.

Display selection follows the argument. This does not require novelty on every
page, a ban on bullets, fixed numbers of reasons, or uniform consulting-style
cards. A complete scientific figure can contain related panels; changing its
artwork remains a separate plotting task. Short English cues and the existing
blank-template preference come from the user's workflow, not from these sources.

The external repository was consulted for ideas, not copied as implementation.
Its MIT LICENSE conflicts with a proprietary frontmatter label and missing
LICENSE.txt reference. Its prose and examples also disagree on divider lines and
minimum font sizes. We did not import its metadata, code, mandatory references
ending, confirmation gates or in-slide plot reconstruction. Its test suggesting
that a meaningful title makes a figure unnecessary is replaced by the relevant
question: does the displayed evidence actually support the title?

Behavioral and artifact checks are recorded in [skill trials](skill-trials.md).
The observed weakness and repair are not a controlled old/new performance study.
